# Sorghum panicle source library

Collected 2026-08-06 for the SorghumProject panicle work. This is a small, local research database: use the two synthesis notes for prompt-ready context, the PDFs for primary detail, and the catalogs whenever an external image or paper is reused.

## Start here

- `research_synthesis.md` — developmental biology, growth phases, reproductive physiology, and modeling implications.
- `maricopa_adaptation.md` — what transfers to the project's Maricopa context, what must be measured locally, and a practical observation protocol.
- `metadata/source_catalog.csv` — provenance, local availability, and direct source links for every literature/data source.
- `metadata/photo_catalog.csv` — attribution and license for every visual reference; eight high-value Commons images are stored locally and the original-file URLs are retained for full-resolution retrieval.

## Contents

- `papers/` — optional local, git-ignored copies of seven openly accessible PDFs. The ICRISAT guide and development chapter are broad field references; the other PDFs concentrate on spikelet fertility, floral/anther development, kernel capacity, Maricopa phenomics, and heat stress.
- `photos/` — optional local, git-ignored reference photography spanning whole-plant heading, exposed panicles, compact/open head forms, branch detail, and individual flower/spikelet detail. The catalog retains original-file URLs and license information.
- `metadata/` — source and photo catalogs. Local copies retain their original research context; do not strip attribution or license information.

## Evidence and use boundaries

The project presently treats the Maricopa AZMET station as a geographic/weather proxy. The public University of Arizona Maricopa studies in this library are a highly relevant analogue, not proof that the current experiment has the same genotype, planting date, irrigation regime, plot coordinates, or phenology. Recalibrate against the experiment's actual emergence, heading, and anthesis observations.

Panicle structure is genotype- and environment-dependent. In particular, a normal grain sorghum branch terminates in a sessile spikelet flanked by two pedicellate spikelets, but the sessile spikelet is ordinarily the fertile one. Avoid treating every visible floral unit as an identical grain.

## Rights and provenance

The photo catalog records author, Commons source page, original-file URL, and Creative Commons license. The PDF catalog records the official landing page and whether a local source copy exists. Keep the source links with anything published outside this workspace; confirm the underlying source license before redistributing a paper or derivative asset.
